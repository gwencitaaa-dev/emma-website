Dr. Emma Sullivan is a Melbourne-based double bassist, educator and writer, originally from Toowoomba, Queensland. She has served as the Principal Double Bass and Education Manager of the Melbourne Chamber Orchestra since 2009, and regularly performs as a casual musician with the Melbourne Symphony Orchestra and Orchestra Victoria.

Her performance career spans the globe. She has toured Europe with the Melbourne Symphony Orchestra, appeared with the New Zealand Symphony Orchestra at the Beijing Olympic Arts Festival, and traveled across regional Australia with the Australian Chamber Orchestra's ACO2. She has also performed with the Australian World Orchestra, bringing together some of the country's finest musicians from ensembles around the world. As a dedicated chamber musician, Emma has performed with the Australian Octet, Southern Cross Soloists, Melbourne Chamber Players and Inventi Ensemble, and is a frequent guest at festivals including Music by the Springs, the Port Fairy Spring Music Festival and the Bendigo Chamber Music Festival.

"You get the opportunity to not only hear the collective sound, but to truly get to know each individual musical personality. It's the perfect blend of chamber intimacy and large-ensemble power." On performing without a conductor with the Melbourne Chamber Orchestra

Emma completed her foundational studies with Michael Morgan at the Queensland Conservatorium, Jeff Bradetich at the University of North Texas, and Steve Reeves at the Australian National Academy of Music (ANAM), where she was a Fellow in 2009. In 2012, she was awarded the prestigious Peter Mitchell Churchill Fellowship to study in Paris under the renowned French double bassist Thierry Barbé. She holds a Doctorate of Musical Arts, completed in 2018 through Griffith University's Queensland Conservatorium, where her doctoral research, Collaborative Contrabass, focused extensively on chamber music written for the instrument.

Beyond the stage, Emma is a passionate educator. She is the founder of the VCASS Double Bass Day, an annual event that brings together student bassists from across Victoria for masterclasses and massed ensemble performances. She also contributes regular writing and commentary on classical music to the online arts publication CutCommon.

research.

Doctoral research and music journalism exploring the double bass and the artists shaping its future.

Doctor of Musical Arts · Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University · 2017

Collaborative Contrabass: Exploring the Role of the Double Bass in Chamber Music Through Performance

A practice-led doctoral project pairing musicological research with four public chamber recitals, tracing the double bass's role in chamber music from the eighteenth century to newly commissioned works — including a study of the historical Viennese tuning system and its Classical-era repertoire, the instrument's changing place in the Romantic piano quintet, and contemporary duets written for double bass over the last three decades. Supervised by Stephen Emmerson and Peter Roennfeldt.
