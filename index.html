Dr Emma Sullivan is a Melbourne-based double bassist, educator and writer, and the Principal Double Bass and Education Manager of Melbourne Chamber Orchestra, where she has performed since 2009. She is also a regular casual musician with the Melbourne Symphony Orchestra and Orchestra Victoria.

Her performing career has taken her around the world — from touring Europe with the Melbourne Symphony Orchestra in 2014, to appearing with the New Zealand Symphony Orchestra at the 2008 Beijing Olympic Arts Festival, to touring regional Australia with the Australian Chamber Orchestra's ACO2. As a chamber musician she has performed with the Australian Octet, Melbourne Chamber Players and Inventi Ensemble, and at festivals including Music by the Springs and the Port Fairy Spring Music Festival.

"You get the opportunity to not only hear the collective sound, but to also get to know each individual musical personality — the perfect blend of chamber music and larger ensemble playing." On performing without a conductor, Melbourne Chamber Orchestra

Emma studied with Michael Morgan at the Queensland Conservatorium, Jeff Bradetich at the University of North Texas, and Steve Reeves at the Australian National Academy of Music, where she was a fellow in 2009. In 2012 she was awarded the Peter Mitchell Churchill Fellowship to study in Paris with French double bassist Thierry Barbé. She holds a Doctorate of Musical Arts from the Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University, for her research project Collaborative Contrabass, focused on chamber music for the double bass.

Alongside performing, Emma is a passionate educator and the founder of the VCASS Double Bass Day, bringing together student bassists from across Victoria for masterclasses and massed ensemble performance. She also writes on music as a contributor to online magazine CutCommon.
